\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\tresumo{Conclusão: Síntese dos resultados, validação das hipóteses levantadas e trabalhos futuros.}

This work set out to address the high cost barrier that limits access to Big Data infrastructure 
for academic institutions and resource-constrained organisations. The three objectives established 
in Section~\ref{sec:introduction} have been fulfilled as follows. Firstly, the proposed framework 
successfully automates the end-to-end creation and configuration of a Hadoop \emph{cluster} on the 
Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) Free Tier, leveraging Terraform for declarative infrastructure 
provisioning and Ansible for idempotent configuration management. As documented in 
Section~\ref{sec:arch}, the pipeline progresses through three automated phases, resource 
provisioning, operating system bootstrapping via \texttt{cloud-init}, and nominal \emph{cluster} 
deployment through the Ambari REST API, incorporating an automated recovery procedure to address 
anticipated deployment failures. Secondly, the installation processes have been 
documented across three provisioning workflow views and a component mapping table, providing a 
reproducible reference for future deployments. Thirdly, the framework's applicability was 
validated through a practical simulation utilising the Star Schema Benchmark (SSB), as detailed in 
Section~\ref{sec:use-case}.

The empirical evaluation yielded results that partially corroborate the central hypothesis. 
Apache Spark consistently outperformed Apache Hive across all 14 analytical queries, 
with performance deltas frequently exceeding 200 seconds per query iteration. However, the 
anticipated advantage of \emph{in-memory} caching over standard Spark execution proved marginal 
under the tested conditions. This outcome is attributed to the inability to cache the 
\texttt{lineorder} fact table, the largest entity in the star schema at 9.3~GB, within the 
constrained memory envelope of the \emph{cluster}. Furthermore, the physical CPU allocation 
established in the YARN configuration restricted Spark to a strict maximum of two concurrent 
executors per job, as each of the three NodeManagers provides exactly one virtual core, and the 
Spark Application Master inherently reserves one container. Despite these constraints, 
the platform remained operationally stable throughout every benchmark iteration, completing all 
scheduled workloads without unrecoverable failures.

Nevertheless, the limitations imposed by the OCI Free Tier must be acknowledged. The hard ceiling of 
4~OCPUs and 24~GB of RAM across the entire \emph{cluster} fundamentally restricts both processing 
parallelism and caching capacity. Additionally, whilst the Open Source Data Platform stack executed on 
the ARM microarchitecture, potential behavioural differences compared to x86 
deployments, particularly regarding JVM optimisations and the availability of pre-compiled 
packages, warrant consideration in production-grade environments. Notwithstanding these constraints, 
the results demonstrate that a fully functional, multi-node \emph{Data Lake} encompassing ingestion, 
distributed storage, analytical processing, and governance layers can be sustained at zero monetary 
cost. This finding validates the framework's suitability as a reproducible platform for academic 
laboratories and teaching environments.

Future work should explore several avenues to extend the contributions presented herein. The benchmark evaluation could 
be expanded to encompass real-time ingestion pipelines, utilising NiFi and Kafka in conjunction with 
Spark Structured Streaming, to validate the Default installation profile under sustained 
streaming workloads. Performance improvements may be investigated through the adoption of columnar 
storage formats such as ORC or Parquet, combined with predicate pushdown optimisations, to reduce 
the I/O overhead observed in the disk-bound phases. Horizontal scalability could be assessed by 
federating multiple Free Tier tenancies or by migrating to paid ARM instances with higher virtual 
core allocations, thereby alleviating the executor bottleneck identified in this study. Finally, 
the integration of Apache Atlas for automated metadata cataloguing would strengthen the governance 
layer, mitigating the risk of \emph{data swamp} scenarios as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:literature}.
