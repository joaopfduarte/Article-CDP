\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work addressed the high cost barrier that limits access to Big Data
infrastructure for academic institutions and resource-constrained organisations.
The three research objectives stated in Section~\ref{sec:introduction} have been
met as follows.

First, the proposed framework automates the end-to-end creation and
configuration of a Hadoop cluster on the Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) Free
Tier, using Terraform for declarative provisioning and Ansible for idempotent
configuration management. As documented in Section~\ref{sec:arch}, the pipeline
proceeds through three phases, resource provisioning, OS bootstrapping via
\texttt{cloud-init}, and cluster deployment through the Ambari REST
API, incorporating an automated recovery procedure that retries failed
installations up to three times. Second, the deployment process has been
documented through three provisioning workflow diagrams and two component-mapping
tables, providing a reproducible reference for future deployments. Third, the
framework's applicability was validated through the SSB, as detailed in Section~\ref{sec:use-case}.

The benchmark results partially corroborate the initial hypothesis. Spark
consistently outperformed Hive across all 14 SSB queries, with mean speed-up
margins ranging from 282 to 2{,}112~seconds per query. The anticipated advantage
of in-memory caching, however, did not materialise as expected. For simpler
queries (Q1.1, Q1.2a, Q1.2b, Q1.3, Q2.1--Q2.3, Q3.3), the caching overhead actually degraded performance by
50--97~seconds relative to uncached Spark, because the 9.3~GB
\texttt{lineorder} fact table could not be fully cached into the cluster's
limited memory. Only the more complex multi-join queries (Q3.1, Q3.2, Q3.4--Q4.3)
benefited from partial caching of the smaller dimension tables, with Q3.2 showing
the largest improvement at 297~seconds. On average across all queries, the
cached configuration was 18~seconds slower than uncached Spark. These findings
suggest that, under severe memory constraints, caching is counterproductive
unless the query complexity is sufficient to amortise the shuffle and spill
overhead.

The platform remained operationally stable throughout all query executions,
completing every iteration without unrecoverable failures. This confirms that a
multi-node Hadoop cluster with distributed storage, analytical processing, and
governance services can be sustained at zero monetary cost on the OCI Free Tier with limited performance.

\subsection*{Limitations}

Several limitations should be noted. The benchmark was conducted on a fixed four-node cluster, 
and without an x86 baseline for direct architectural comparison. Furthermore, the two Spark
configurations differ not only in caching behaviour but also in memory
allocation and shuffle settings (Section~\ref{sec:use-case}), introducing a
confounding variable that prevents the caching effect from being fully
isolated. The hard ceiling of 4~OCPUs and
24~GB of RAM fundamentally restricts both processing parallelism and caching
capacity. Additionally, whilst the ODP stack executed on the ARM
microarchitecture without critical failures, potential behavioural differences
compared to x86 deployments, particularly regarding JVM optimisations and the
availability of pre-compiled packages, warrant consideration before adopting
the platform in production-grade environments.

\subsection*{Future Work}

Several directions merit further investigation. The evaluation could be
extended to real-time ingestion pipelines using NiFi and Kafka in conjunction
with Spark Structured Streaming, validating the Default profile under sustained
streaming workloads. Performance may improve through columnar storage formats
such as ORC or Parquet combined with predicate-pushdown optimisations, reducing
the I/O overhead observed in the disk-bound phases. A controlled comparison
between the ARM-based free-tier cluster and an equivalent x86 deployment would
isolate architectural effects from resource-constraint effects, addressing one
of the principal limitations of the present study. Horizontal scalability could
be assessed by federating multiple Free Tier tenancies or by migrating to paid
ARM instances with higher virtual core allocations, alleviating the executor
bottleneck identified in this study.
